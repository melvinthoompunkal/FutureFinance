1.1 WHAT THE TOOL SHOULD BE
A "context and levels" dashboard, not a prediction machine. It answers:
  - What regime is the market in?   (VIX level and term structure, index trend, breadth, credit)
  - How is this stock's sector doing relative to the market?
  - How healthy is this stock, where are its key levels, how wide is the plausible
    range, and is options positioning likely to damp or amplify moves?
It never answers "will it go up". Nothing free and honest can.

1.2 RECOMMENDED INDICATOR SET (the short list that earns its place)
  REGIME (market-wide):   SPY/QQQ vs 50/200 DMA, VIX level + own-history percentile,
                          VIX/VIX3M ratio, CBOE equity and index put/call as percentiles,
                          HY credit spread (or HYG/IEF ratio), breadth (% above 50/200 DMA)
  SECTOR:                 sector ETF relative strength vs SPY, RRG-style quadrant,
                          stock vs sector vs market relative strength (3 levels)
  TREND/LEVELS (price):   price vs 20/50/200 SMA, ADX, ATR and ATR%, Bollinger bandwidth,
                          52-week high/low distance, volume profile POC/value area (from
                          intraday bars if available), RVOL, swing support/resistance
  OPTIONS/DEALERS:        net GEX by strike split by expiry bucket, zero-gamma flip
                          (recomputed), call wall, put wall, net-to-gross GEX ratio,
                          expected move (ATM straddle and S*IV*sqrt(DTE/365)),
                          IV term structure, 25-delta skew, IV vs HV, put/call by OI
                          and volume, bid/ask spread % as a quality filter, OI change
                          (needs your own nightly archive)
  VOLATILITY:             IV rank and percentile (needs archive), HV20/HV60, VRP
                          (IV minus forward realized, shown historically), earnings implied move
  FUNDAMENTALS (veto):    growth, margins, FCF yield, leverage, interest coverage,
                          valuation vs own 5y history and vs sector median, next earnings
                          date, last 8 surprises, Form 4 insider activity, short interest
  FLOW (low weight):      FINRA short interest (lagged), short volume (labeled as such),
                          ATS volume share (labeled "no side"), analyst consensus + revisions

1.3 TEN FINDINGS THAT CHANGE THE DESIGN
  1. Gamma has two axes (sign x magnitude): SUPPORTED. But "near-zero gamma is the
     worst case" is NOT established. Naive-GEX studies mostly show a MONOTONIC
     relationship (more negative = wider next day), while one position-based study
     (OptionMetrics, via Risk.net) found volatility peaks near zero. Show both,
     claim neither. [V S13,S14,S16,S21,S22]
  2. The vendor 8-year SPY backtest is real and says what you said: rho -0.36 raw,
     -0.14 after VIX, -0.03 (p=0.18) after VIX + ATM IV. It is one vendor, one ETF,
     EOD snapshots, linear controls. A second source (Build Alpha) disagrees on
     whether GEX adds info beyond recent realized vol. Treat GEX as a REGIME LABEL
     that mostly overlaps VIX, not as an independent alpha signal. [V S21,S22]
  3. No study I found supports GEX predicting price DIRECTION. SpotGamma's own docs
     say it cannot answer up vs down. DEX showed no next-day return signal. [V S21,S24]
  4. SIGN CONVENTION: your note is partly wrong. SqueezeMetrics' original and the
     common SpotGamma-style formula use the SAME mapping (calls long gamma for dealers,
     puts short gamma). The opposite-sign implementations are "customers are net
     buyers" models (dealers short calls, long puts) and trade-classified/flow models.
     [V S23,S24,S25]
  5. Robinhood: "only crypto is official" is OUT OF DATE. Since May 27, 2026 Robinhood
     offers Agentic Trading through an MCP server, but in a SEPARATE dedicated account
     for AI agents, not read access to your main portfolio. So your conclusion
     (optional, read-only, unofficial route for your watchlist) still stands. [V S5]
  6. Pine Script: correct that it cannot fetch arbitrary URLs. But Pine has
     request.seed() which reads EOD CSV data from a GitHub repo ("Pine Seeds"), updated
     only a few times per day. A nightly git push of levels could feed a Pine
     indicator without manual pasting. Check current onboarding rules first. [V S6][?]
  7. FRED's ICE BofA OAS series (HY OAS BAMLH0A0HYM2) is truncated to 3 years since
     April 2026, and FRED's S&P 500 series is limited to 10 years. VIXCLS and VXVCLS
     (VIX3M) are still there. Archive HY spreads yourself or use a price-ratio proxy. [V S12]
  8. IV Rank/Percentile, OI change, GEX history, put/call history cannot be backfilled
     for free. CBOE's free put/call CSVs stop at 2019-10-04. So the nightly archiver
     IS the MVP, not Phase 2. [V S3,S38]
  9. 0DTE was 59% of SPX volume in 2025 and hit a record 66.2% in July 2026, but
     volume is not gamma: Cboe found dealer net 0DTE gamma small because customer flow
     is balanced, and OI-based GEX cannot see positions opened and closed intraday.
     Always split GEX by expiry bucket. [V S18,S26]
  10. Price indicators are transforms of one OHLCV series. Ten agreeing = one piece
     of evidence. Group by family, never average into one "bullish score". [K]

SECTION 3. THE GAMMA QUESTION
================================================================================

3.1 MECHANICS [K]
  - A dealer delta-hedges. If dealers are LONG gamma, their delta rises as price
    rises, so they SELL into rallies and BUY dips. Counter-trend, damps volatility.
  - If dealers are SHORT gamma they BUY rallies and SELL dips. Pro-trend, amplifies.
  - Black-Scholes gamma (per share): G = exp(-qT) * phi(d1) / (S * sigma * sqrt(T)).
    It is highest at the money and for short maturities, and FALLS as sigma rises.
  - Dollar GEX per 1% move at strike i: G_i * OI_i * 100 * sign_i * S^2 * 0.01.
    SpotGamma's published formula is the same shape. [V S24]

3.2 THE TWO-AXIS FRAMEWORK (sign x magnitude): SUPPORTED, WITH CAVEATS
  - Large positive: stabilizing / mean-reverting. Large negative: amplifying /
    momentum. Evidence below.
  - Near-zero |net gamma|: "no anchor" is a reasonable reading (little net hedging
    flow in either direction, and spot is near the flip so the sign can change on a
    small move). But the claim that this is WORSE than negative gamma is contested.

3.3 EVIDENCE TABLE
  SUPPORTS THE CONDITIONAL STORY
  - Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (JFE 2021, vol 142, 377-403): intraday market
    momentum (return in the last 30 minutes predicted by the rest of the day) is
    linked to gamma hedging demand. Using an S&P 500 option proxy for negative gamma
    exposure (NGE), momentum is present when NGE is negative and stronger when more
    negative; on positive-NGE days there is no significant intraday momentum. [V S13]
    NOTE: this is continuation of the day's move, conditional on regime. Not an
    unconditional direction signal.
  - Barbon and Buraschi, "Gamma Fragility" (working paper, rev. 2021): large aggregate
    dealer gamma imbalances link to intraday momentum (negative) and reversal
    (positive), explain intraday volatility and autocorrelation, and relate to
    flash-crash frequency and size. Effect stronger for less liquid underlyings. [V S14]
  - Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi, Moerke (2021): large negative (positive) gamma
    imbalance relative to dollar volume gives end-of-day momentum (reversal). [V S14]
  - Ni, Pearson, Poteshman (JFE 2005, 78(1) 49-87): stock prices cluster at strikes on
    expiration dates; hedge rebalancing by option market makers and manipulation both
    contribute. Secondary sources cite at least ~16.5 bp of return impact per
    expiration (1996-2002). [V S15][?]
  - Adams et al. (SSRN 5641974, via Quantpedia): S&P 500 volatility about 60 annualized
    bp lower on days with 0DTE trading; market maker hedging is the proposed channel
    (market makers hold larger net gamma on those days). [V S19]
  - OptionMetrics (DeSimone, via Risk.net): volatility is greatest when dealer gamma
    is flat (near zero), using position-based estimates. [V S16]
  LIMITS THE STORY
  - Cboe (Mandy Xu, 2023): despite huge SPX 0DTE volume, customer flow is balanced so
    market maker net 0DTE gamma is small; implied hedging is about 1.3% to 1.9% of S&P
    futures daily notional, "hardly the tail wagging the dog". Cboe is an interested
    party. [V S18]
  - Princeton senior thesis (2026): canonical gamma hedging channel adds no meaningful
    predictive power for intraday variance forecasts (QLIKE and rank correlations). Student
    work, treat as a flag not a verdict. [V S20]
  - Vasquez, Amaya, Pearson, Garcia-Ares (2025, Cboe Options Institute grant): estimates
    the MAXIMUM impact of option-market-maker gamma on index volatility from proprietary
    trade data. A secondary citation says actual dealer gamma is positive on almost
    every session, i.e. naive GEX is often wrong about the sign. I read only the
    abstract. [V S17][?]
  - FlashAlpha 8-year SPY backtest (below). [V S21]

3.4 THE VIX-PROXY WEAKNESS: CONFIRMED
  FlashAlpha (vendor of a GEX product) tested its own data: 1,972 SPY end-of-day
  snapshots, 2018-2026, next-day realized vol proxied by |log return| * sqrt(252).
  Spearman correlations of GEX with next-day RV:
     raw                      -0.36  (p ~ 5e-60)
     controlling VIX          -0.14  (p = 1.2e-9)
     controlling VIX + ATM IV -0.03  (p = 0.18, not significant)
  DEX vs next-day return: ~0 (top-minus-bottom 0.00%, t=0.04). VEX vs next-day ATM IV
  change: -0.16 raw, -0.01 (p=0.77) after both controls. CHEX fragile. [V S21]
  Caveats: single ETF, EOD snapshots (intraday claims untested), linear residual
  controls, and one-day |return| is a very noisy volatility proxy (low power).
  Counter-evidence: Build Alpha (SPX options, 2004-2026) finds the lowest GEX quintile
  has ~3x the next-day range of the highest, monotonic across quintiles, and that
  normalizing by the prior 20-day ATR shrinks but keeps the effect (rank corr -0.20).
  That controls for recent REALIZED vol, not for IMPLIED vol, so it does not
  contradict FlashAlpha directly. [V S22]
  Mechanical reason for the overlap: BS gamma falls as sigma rises, and spot-IV and
  dealer positioning co-move, so a GEX magnitude series partly re-expresses VIX. [K]
  DESIGN CONSEQUENCE: always show VIX/ATM IV next to GEX. Label GEX a regime context.

3.6 SIGN CONVENTIONS (your note, corrected)
  Convention A (SqueezeMetrics 2016/2017 white paper; SpotGamma-style public formula):
    Assume investors overwrite calls (dealers BUY calls) and buy puts for protection
    (dealers SELL puts). Therefore dealers: calls = LONG gamma (+), puts = SHORT gamma (-).
    net GEX = sum( call GEX ) - sum( put GEX ). SpotGamma calls this "a simplifying
    inventory convention, not a rule of option mathematics". [V S23,S24]
    NOTE: "SpotGamma-style (calls +, puts -)" and "SqueezeMetrics (dealers long calls,
    short puts)" are the SAME mapping. They do not conflict.
  Convention B ("customers are net buyers"): dealers short calls AND long puts, so
    calls = (-), puts = (+). It flips every sign vs A. Used in some open-source models
    (e.g. a whitepaper assuming "dealers are net short options: short calls, long puts"
    and a quant-lessons repo coding calls -1, puts +1). [V S25]
  Convention C (flow/trade-classified): infer dealer position from today's classified
    trades; can disagree with A on a given day. [V S25]
  Confusion in the wild: one MQL5 tutorial states dealers are short calls and long puts,
  then treats calls as positive anyway. Check every library you copy. [V S25]
  RECOMMENDATION
    - Implement A as the default, label it "ASSUMED dealer positioning".
    - Show gross call GEX and gross put GEX separately, and a NET-TO-GROSS RATIO
      = |net| / (call_GEX + |put_GEX|). Low ratio = the net sign is a small difference
      of big numbers and flips under tiny assumption changes. Caption: "sign low confidence".
    - Single stocks: retail call buying breaks the "customers overwrite calls" premise, so
      sign confidence is lower than for SPX/SPY/QQQ. [K]
    - A toggle for convention B is fine but it just negates everything; its value is
      pedagogical.

3.7 THE EXPIRY PROBLEM
  - 0DTE share of SPX volume: 59% (2025), 66.2% (July 2026). [V S26]
  - Volume share is not OI or gamma share. Newly listed dailies carry little overnight OI,
    and positions opened and closed intraday never appear in OI-based GEX. [V S38][K]
  - Split every GEX view into buckets: 0DTE, 1-7 DTE (weekly), 8-45 DTE (monthly cycle),
    >45 DTE (LEAPS-ish). Report "share of total gamma from the nearest expiry" (SpotGamma
    publishes such a metric). Levels that depend on one expiring bucket are flagged
    "expires today/this week". [V S24][K]

3.8 ZERO-GAMMA FLIP: HOW TO COMPUTE [K]
  The published gamma is evaluated at current spot. The flip is the spot S* where total
  net gamma crosses zero, so you must re-price:
    1. Grid of hypothetical spots, e.g. S0 * [0.85 ... 1.15] in 0.25% steps.
    2. For every contract with valid IV, OI > 0: d1(S) = (ln(S/K) + (r - q + 0.5 s^2) T) /
       (s sqrt(T)); gamma(S) = exp(-qT) phi(d1) / (S s sqrt(T)). Hold each contract's IV
       fixed (sticky-strike assumption; state it). T measured to 16:00 ET of expiry,
       with a floor (e.g. 1/(252*6.5) years) so 0DTE does not explode.
    3. net(S) = sum_i sign_i * gamma_i(S) * OI_i * 100 * S^2 * 0.01.
    4. Flip = linear interpolation at sign change nearest to spot. Handle zero crossings
       (report all, mark the one nearest spot) and no crossing (report "none in range").
    5. Plot net(S) vs S: this "gamma profile curve" is the honest signature chart.
  r from FRED DGS3MO or a constant; q from dividend yield or 0 (state it).

3.9 REGIME LABELS (use percentiles of own history, never fixed dollar thresholds)
    STABILIZING:  net GEX high positive percentile AND spot above flip
    AMPLIFYING:   net GEX negative (or low percentile) AND spot below flip
    UNANCHORED:   |net GEX| in low percentile OR spot within ~0.5 expected-move of flip
    CONFLICTED:   expiry buckets disagree in sign, or net-to-gross ratio low
  Each label ships with: as-of time, OI date, convention, and the caption
  "Describes how moves may be damped or amplified. Does NOT predict direction."
